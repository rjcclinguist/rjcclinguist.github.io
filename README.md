## About me
### Interests
Theoretical and laboratory phonology, psycholinguistics, autosegmental theory, psychology of bilingualism, speech perception, poetic meters, and the prosody-music interface.

### My research
I'm Ray, a master's student in theoretical linguistics at Université de Montréal, since 2025.

I specialize in phonology: the sound systems of natural language. Phonologists look to build abstract mathematical models that represent how sounds are stored, organized, and processed in our brains. Specifically, when a speaker builds a sentence, I'm interested in how they organize the abstract sounds in their head, and translate them into a sequence of muscular commands that their bodies can then articulate. I use phonetic data (from speech acoustics) and psycholinguistic data (from language cognition) to make predictions about the sound patterns of different languages.

My most recent work focused on consonants in Chilean Spanish. The /s/ sound at the end of a word can be deleted and determine the realization of the following consonant at the beginning of the next word. However, the type of boundary between the two words plays a role in supporting or blocking this effect. I also study the connection between linguistic structure and music and poetry. How speech is transferred into a melody or a poetic meter gives us clues toward the rhythmic and tonal properties of languages. Other topics I have investigated include the differences between tone and stress, the interpretation of plural marking on adjectives, and the distribution of R-sounds in Romance languages.

Overall, my expertise lie in the musical aspects of language and the interaction between sounds. With this experience in linguistics and music, I approach my research with the scrutiny of a scientist and the tact of an artist.
